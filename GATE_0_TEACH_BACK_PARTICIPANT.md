## Reading

This project asks whether a word-only lexical-coverage report can hide a
reading burden created by a multiword expression (MWE).

Consider: *At first the explanation was confusing, but she took it in.* A
reader may know the individual words *take*, *it*, and *in* but not understand
the contextual meaning of *take ... in*. A conventional word-token profile may
therefore mark all three member words as known even though the reader does not
know the expression in this occurrence.

The method keeps three reference relationships separate:

1. **List-to-text:** the proportion of eligible word tokens in a text that
   match a named vocabulary list and cutoff. This is hypothetical,
   list-conditioned coverage; it does not show what a participant knows.
2. **Source-corpus-to-target-corpus:** the proportion of eligible target-corpus
   items meeting declared occurrence and range rules in a source corpus. This
   describes directional recurrence; it does not show exposure, learning, or
   comprehension.
3. **Tested person-to-text:** for one participant and one text, individual word
   meanings demonstrated for eligible word tokens (`P_word`) and contextual
   MWE meanings demonstrated for eligible confirmed MWE occurrences
   (`P_mwe_sense`). These are reported as separate quantities. Demonstrated
   lexical knowledge alone does not guarantee or cause comprehension.

For `P_word`, the denominator is the eligible word tokens in the text, and MWE
members remain individually scored. For `P_mwe_sense`, the denominator is the
sense-eligible, researcher-confirmed MWE occurrences. Missing, ambiguous, and
unresolved decisions remain visible rather than being counted as known.

The Web app maps text-side word tokens and reviewable MWE occurrences, records
human decisions about their members, idiomaticity, and contextual sense, and
exports an auditable record. It does not test participants, administer reading
comprehension, infer proficiency, or decide MWE truth automatically. Separate
study procedures collect participant word knowledge, contextual MWE knowledge,
and comprehension and join them to the exported text map.

The historical 95% and 98% figures are reference points for interpreting
word-token coverage, not universal comprehension guarantees. The project will
not turn an unknown MWE into one, two, or three unknown words, subtract its
members from word coverage, create a combined adjusted percentage, or propose a
new threshold unless independent criterion evidence supports such a rule.

## Questions

1. What problem is the project trying to measure? Explain it using *took it in*.
2. How do list-to-text, source-corpus-to-target-corpus, and tested
   person-to-text profiles differ? State one inference that each profile does
   **not** permit.
3. What is counted in the denominator of `P_word`, and what is counted in the
   denominator of `P_mwe_sense`? What happens to unresolved cases?
4. What do 95% and 98% mean in this project, and what do they not guarantee?
5. What does the Web app do, and what must the separate participant study do?
6. On the evidence described here, may the project report one combined
   word-and-MWE coverage percentage or claim a new universal threshold? Why or
   why not?
7. A list-to-text profile matches every word in *The deal fell through at the
   last minute.* What may the researcher report, and what additional evidence
   would be needed before saying that a particular reader understood the
   expression in this sentence?
